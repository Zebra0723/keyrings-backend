The Finding Joy series

reading guide

A starting point for book clubs and classrooms. A fuller guide arrives with the books.

themes to explore

  • Courage and fear — what bravery actually costs
  • Family, distance, and the objects that stand in for people
  • Exile and belonging — home as a place you build, not inherit
  • Sport as identity, weapon, and refuge
  • Voice — how first-person narration shapes what we know

These notes are drawn from the Book 1 preview and will expand into a full guide at release.

discussion questions

  1. Laleh calls her racket "my partner, my weapon, my everything." What does the racket come to represent, and how does that meaning change across the match?
  2. The story is told entirely in the first person. How would it feel different if we could also hear Sinclair's or Farah's point of view?
  3. At the hardest moment, Laleh clutches her mother's medallion. Why do small objects carry so much weight in this story?
  4. Laleh learns tennis in secret, at real risk. What does the book suggest about why some things are worth that danger?
  5. Momentum swings back and forth in the match. Where did you feel the turning point, and what changed for Laleh internally?
  6. The series deals with exile and belonging. What does "home" mean to Laleh — and does it stay the same?
  7. Ahmed napping on the couch appears in a flash at the tensest moment. Why might that ordinary image matter so much?
  8. How does the title — Finding Joy — reframe a story about struggle and loss?
  9. The second book turns on "one letter, fifteen years, and the only name I knew was Laleh." What do you predict it asks Laleh to confront?
  10. If you could ask Laleh one question at the end of the sample chapter, what would it be?
