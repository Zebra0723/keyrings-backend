The Finding Joy series

the story

A girl, a forbidden game, and the long road from Kabul to center court.

In Kabul, tennis is forbidden to girls. Laleh plays anyway — before dawn, behind walls, with a borrowed racket and the sound of her own heartbeat for a crowd. What begins as a stolen secret becomes the one thing worth risking everything for.

the game

For Laleh, the racket is never just equipment. It is a partner with a faded grip, strings dyed yellow by a thousand practice balls, a frame lacerated but loyal. In a world determined to make her small, the court is the one place she is allowed to be enormous — to swing, to shout, to want. The sport becomes both her weapon and her refuge, and the series never lets you forget the cost of either.

the journey

When the danger closes in, Laleh and her family are forced to run. Carrying little more than her mother's medallion and that battered racket, she chases an impossible dream across borders, courts, and continents. The trilogy follows her from a hidden game in Afghanistan to the roar of a professional stadium — and, in the second book, back toward the people and the past that exile scattered.

the themes

Courage that looks like stubbornness. Family carried in a medallion and a memory. Exile, and the ache of belonging to two places and neither. Told entirely in the first person, Finding Joy asks what it means to build a home out of a game — and what you are willing to fight for once you find your joy.
